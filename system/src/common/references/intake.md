# Business, buyer and research intake

Start from the current request and known project files. If several products or clients exist, identify the one the user means before combining evidence. A website's language is a clue, not proof of the desired sales market.

## Minimum viable brief

For research: product/service and offer category; destination or enough product facts; buyer/use situation; selling geography; ad language; purpose of the scan. Also establish an absolute date interval or a disclosed recommended window, active/all status and a bounded sample. Missing essential identity, market or language blocks a targeted scrape; ask one compact batch. Existing answers remain valid unless contradicted.

For copy: add the action/landing destination, offer details, meaningful differentiator, substantiated proof, restrictions and desired format. For production: add approved product assets, visual identity, dimensions and aspect ratios, target duration, whether there is narration and in which voice and accent, music or sound direction, provider access and generation ceiling. For launch: add the account and Page, objective/event, budget/currency/timezone, run dates, destination, measurement and action authorization.

Example question, translated to the user's language: "What do you sell, who should buy it, and in which country/language? A product URL is enough to start; I will extract the rest and flag uncertainties."

Do not ask a 40-field questionnaire before a useful first draft. Mark optional unknowns and propose reasonable assumptions. Never infer sensitive traits about individual people. Use aggregate, relevant buying contexts and stated needs.

## Avatar as a decision model

Create a buyer card with: job they need done; triggering moment; current workaround; obstacle; desired change; awareness of alternatives; decision criteria; objections; vocabulary with sources; valid exclusion/poor-fit conditions; buying authority. Demographics belong only when supported and decision-relevant. For B2B separate user, buyer and approver. For local services separate service area, project type, eligibility and lead qualification.

Represent competing hypotheses: a buyer who already uses a workaround needs different proof from someone who has not identified the problem. Do not invent a biography to make a persona feel researched. Store evidence strength beside each conclusion.

## The register question, which is blocking

One question must be asked rather than assumed, in every pack: **what register is this brand allowed to speak in?** Serious and restrained, native and informal, cinematic, documentary, or deliberately unpolished. A law firm and a snack brand sell on the same platform and cannot use the same grammar. Getting this wrong produces a technically excellent creative the client cannot publish, which is a total loss rather than a partial one.

The stakes are higher in the motion packs, because a register mistake in a video is carried by the voice, the pacing and the cut as well as by the words, and none of that can be edited out afterwards. It is the same question in the still packs and it costs the same minute to ask.

If the user does not know, show three examples from the research pull and let them point. That takes a minute and settles it for good. Record the answer in the brief.

## Research windows

Use these as proposals, not platform guarantees: 7 days for a rapid trend scan; 30 days for a current creative landscape; 90 days for evergreen variation; the same seasonal period in the previous year when available. Give absolute start/end dates and timezone. For a launch tomorrow, prioritize current offers and active creatives. For high-consideration services, a broader window may capture more variation. The provider may expose creation/start dates rather than the complete historical delivery interval; record the distinction. If the window cannot be honored, disclose the coverage gap rather than silently switching to all-time.

Deliver a short confirmed brief, unknowns that affect output, research settings and the next useful artifact. Save only what will help the next task.
