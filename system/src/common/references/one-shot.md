# One pass, done right

The first delivery is the deliverable. Not a draft to be corrected, not a
proposal to be approved, not half the work with a question attached. Either it
is usable as it stands, or it names precisely what stopped it and what it would
take to finish.

This is the most demanding rule in the pack, and it is not a rule about
working harder. It is a rule about the order of operations. Everything that
breaks a first pass breaks it before the first sentence is written.

---

## The three ways a first pass dies

**A missing input you did not ask for.** You needed the price, the guarantee,
the destination URL, the register. You started anyway, guessed, and produced
something built on a wrong assumption. The work is not partly right. It is
entirely rebuilt.

**An assumption you made in silence.** You did not have the margin, so you
picked a plausible target cost per acquisition. Nothing in the delivery says
so. The reader acts on a number you invented, and neither of you knows it.

**A deliverable shipped in pieces.** The image is there and the copy is not.
The video is rendered and the captions are missing. Each piece looks like
progress and none of them is usable, because an ad is not a set of parts.

Everything below exists to close those three.

---

## Ask everything at once, before anything

**Never interview.** Asking one question, receiving one answer, then asking the
next is the slowest and most annoying shape this work can take, and it is what
turns a one-hour job into three days of messages.

Read [intake](intake.md), decide the complete list of what is genuinely
blocking, and ask for all of it in a single message. Group it, number it, and
say for each item what happens if it stays unanswered.

The blocking set is small and it does not change much:

| Item | Why it blocks | If it stays unanswered |
|---|---|---|
| Offer in exact terms: price, what is included, guarantee, delivery | Every claim and every CTA depends on it | Stop. Nothing downstream is safe. |
| Market and ad language | Copy is written in the market's language, never translated into it | Stop. |
| Register, per [intake](intake.md) | Decides vocabulary, pace, imagery | Stop. A wrong register is a total loss. |
| Destination and what happens there | The ad promises what the page must keep | Proceed, state the assumption, mark the continuity check as unrun |
| Proof available: results, reviews, tests, credentials | Decides which claims are allowed at all | Proceed with claims reduced to what is provable, and say so |
| Hard prohibitions: words, competitors, legal terms | The red line in [compliance](compliance.md) | Proceed on category defaults and say the list was not supplied |

The right column matters as much as the left. A question that does not block
should not be asked before starting; it should be answered by a reasonable
default, stated in the delivery.

---

## State every assumption where it is read, not where it is buried

An assumption at the bottom of a long document has not been communicated.

Put them at the top of the delivery, in their own short block, phrased as what
you did and what would change if it is wrong:

> Assumed a contribution margin of 60% because none was supplied. The target
> cost per acquisition below follows from it. If the real margin is 40%, that
> target drops by a third and two of the three angles stop being worth testing.

One line each. If there are more than four, you skipped the intake step.

---

## Ship every channel the format has

A deliverable is complete when every channel a human will encounter is
present. Missing channels are not omissions to be noticed later; they are the
reason the work gets sent back.

| Format | Channels that must all be present |
|---|---|
| Static ad | Exported image at every requested ratio, the on-image text as a copy manifest, primary text, headline, description, the CTA, the destination, the claims list with its proof |
| Carousel | The above for every card, plus each card's role in the argument and its destination |
| Video ad | The file, captions, a transcript, the script, the voice and settings manifest, the music and rights note, the thumbnail frame, the same copy fields as a static |
| Copy only | Every requested variant, each with its angle named, its claims listed and its character counts against the limits |

When a channel is genuinely impossible, the delivery says which one and why, in
one sentence, at the top. **Silence is the failure.** Shipping seven of eight
channels without mentioning the eighth is worse than shipping none, because it
looks finished.

---

## Never narrow the scope quietly

If the request was three angles and you produce two, say so. If the request was
a video and no video capability exists, say that, and deliver everything up to
that wall, per [runtime](runtime.md) and the pack's providers file.

Scaling the work down is the user's decision, never yours. Your job is to make
the trade visible: here is what exists, here is what is missing, here is the
cheapest way to get it.

---

## The self-check before handing over

Run this against the actual delivery, not against your memory of writing it.

1. Could someone launch this today without asking me a single question?
2. Is every number in it either sourced or labelled as an assumption?
3. Did I open every exported file, or am I trusting that generation succeeded?
4. Does every claim have a proof id, and does every proof id exist?
5. Is there any sentence that would need a follow-up message to be actionable?
6. Did the request ask for anything I did not deliver and did not mention?

A no anywhere is not a note for later. It is the work not being finished.

Then run [quality control](quality-control.md), which is the pass that decides
whether the answers above are true rather than intended.
