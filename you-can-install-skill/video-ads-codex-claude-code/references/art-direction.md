# Art direction: choosing a look instead of having one

A pack with a house style produces ads that look like the pack. Every client gets
the same bold sans-serif on the same off-white, the same 4 percent scale bounce, the
same three icons. It reads as competent and it is invisible, because the viewer has
seen it eleven times today.

So this file is a chooser, not a template. It says what the options are, what each
one is good at, what it costs, and how to pick against a specific brand.

---

## Choosing

Four inputs decide, in this order:

1. **The brand.** What already exists: typeface, colour, photography, tone. An ad
   that does not look like the brand is a stranger asking for money.
2. **The audience.** What they already trust and what already reads as an ad to them.
3. **The offer.** A demonstration wants to be shown. A number wants to be composed.
   An idea wants to be told.
4. **The channel.** Sound-off feed, sound-on reels, a placement that overlays its
   own interface across the bottom fifth.

Then write the choice into the manifest with one sentence saying why. A direction
picked without a reason is a taste, and it will be argued with forever.

## The directions

| Direction | Strongest when | Costs | Fails as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kinetic typography** | The argument is verbal, a number or a promise carries it, no product is worth showing | Needs real typographic craft or it reads as a template | Words zooming for no reason |
| **Editorial collage** | Brand has photography or archive material, wants a considered feel | Slow to build, needs a real image bank | A mood board with no argument |
| **Interface or data motion** | The product is software, or the proof is a number that moved | Fake interfaces read as fake instantly | A dashboard nobody can read at thumbnail size |
| **Mixed media** | Standing out in a category that all looks the same | Easy to make incoherent | Random textures pretending to be a style |
| **2D vector** | Explaining a process or a mechanism | Generic by default | Corporate-memphis characters shaking hands |
| **2.5D layered** | Wanting depth and camera movement without 3D cost | Parallax overused turns seasick | Everything drifting all the time |
| **Augmented live footage** | Real material exists and needs structure on top | Requires the footage to be good first | Motion papering over weak footage |
| **Product demonstration** | The product does something visible | Needs the product in hand and honest lighting | A silent turntable nobody watches |
| **Documentary** | Trust is the obstacle, the place and the people are the proof | Needs real access and real people | Staged spontaneity, which everyone spots |
| **Premium minimal** | High price, mature brand, the restraint is the message | One weak frame destroys it | Empty space presented as taste |
| **Direct-response impact** | Cold audience, hard offer, price is the argument | Wears out fast, ages the brand | Shouting with a countdown timer |
| **Motion-enriched UGC** | Native feel plus the clarity of graphics | Two registers that can fight | A phone video with a corporate lower-third |

Directions combine, but only when the combination is decided. Two registers chosen is
a style. Two registers arrived at is a mess.

## What every animation must earn

An animation stays only if it serves at least one of these, and the storyboard says
which:

- **Comprehension.** It makes something easier to understand than a cut would.
- **Hierarchy.** It says what to look at first.
- **Tension.** It creates or resolves an expectation.
- **Proof.** It shows something happening rather than asserting it.
- **Continuity.** It carries the eye from one idea to the next without a jolt.
- **Emotion.** It changes how the moment feels, deliberately.
- **Passage.** It moves the argument to the next idea.

If none of them applies, delete it. The frame gets quieter and the ad gets faster.

## The generic trap

These are the specific shapes that make an ad forgettable. They are not banned. They
are what to do differently when nothing else is decided.

- **Text that zooms, and only zooms.** Scale is one property. Weight, tracking,
  masking, baseline, colour and position all animate too.
- **Abstract shapes with no referent.** Floating circles mean nothing and cost the
  attention of something that would.
- **Generic icons.** A line-art shopping cart is a placeholder that shipped.
- **Every element entering.** If everything moves in, nothing arrived.
- **A stock transition on every cut.** A transition is a sentence about the relation
  between two shots. Most cuts are just a cut.
- **The whole video at one density.** No rest, no impact.

## Building a frame that holds

**Depth.** Foreground, subject, background, with something happening in at least two
of them. A single flat plane is why most motion ads look cheap.

**Camera or parallax when it means something.** A push in adds pressure. A pull out
gives context. Drift for the sake of drift adds nothing and can nauseate.

**Scale contrast.** The one number that matters is the biggest thing in the frame.
Everything the same size is a list, not an argument.

**Density variation.** Dense moments read as information. Sparse moments read as
importance. Alternating them is how attention survives twenty seconds.

**Breathing room.** At least one moment where nothing moves. It is where the viewer
catches up, and it makes the next impact land.

**Impact moments.** Two or three per ad, no more. A frame that stops, a sound that
hits, a scale that jumps. More than three and none of them is an impact.

**Secondary detail.** Something small and right in the corner of the frame. It is the
difference between made and generated, and it costs almost nothing.

**Motivated transitions.** The cut happens because the idea changed, and the
transition says how. Matching on a shape, on a movement, on a sound, or on a colour
all mean something. A random glitch means the editor had a plugin.

## Checking the direction was actually applied

At review, per [creative QA](creative-qa.md):

- Could this frame belong to another brand with the logo swapped? Then the direction
  was named, not applied.
- Does the typographic scale come from the brand or from the default?
- Is there one frame anyone would recognise a day later?
- Does the motion vocabulary repeat with intent, or by accident?
