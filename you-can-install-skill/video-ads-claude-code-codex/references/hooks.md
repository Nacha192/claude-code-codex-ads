# Hooks

A hook is not a clever line. It is the shortest possible proof that this ad is
about the reader's actual situation. Everything else in the creative is
downstream of that proof landing.

---

## A hook is three channels, judged together

| Channel | Static ad | Video ad |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual** | what the image shows before any word is read | the opening shot, first frame |
| **Text** | the burned-in line, and the first line of primary text | the burned-in caption line |
| **Audio** | — | the first spoken words |

A strong spoken line over a shot that shows nothing is a weak hook. A brilliant
burned-in line on an image the eye slides off is a weak hook. Write all three at
once and rank them as a unit.

**Assume sound off** (`voice.sound_off_default`). The text and visual channels
must carry the hook alone. The audio channel is a bonus for the minority with
sound on — never the load-bearing one.

**Do not let the text channel transcribe the audio channel.** Two channels
saying the same thing wastes one of them. The spoken line and the burned-in
line should be doing different jobs: one names the situation, the other names
the stakes, or the turn, or the time.

---

## Before writing: the three inputs

From `ads/brief.md`, not from imagination:

1. **The lived problem** — the situation, physically described. This is the
   hook's subject. Not the workaround, not the product benefit.
2. **The awareness level** this batch targets. It decides whether the hook
   reveals or confirms, and those are opposite movements.
3. **The sentence the buyer says to themselves** — from reviews, from the
   research pull, from the user. Hooks written from this sentence beat hooks
   written from a formula, every time.

If you have the third input, **start there and skip the formula library**. The
library is for when you are empty, not for when you have the raw material.

---

## The moment of recognition

Before writing a single line, name it: **the specific thing the reader sees or
hears that tells them this ad is about them.** One sentence. Write it down.

Every hook in the set is then an attempt to deliver that moment faster.

Hooks written without this step come out generic, because "generic" is exactly
what a line looks like when it is not trying to be recognised by anyone in
particular.

---

## The rule that overrides every formula below

**Describe the situation. Never the person.**

Not "tired of X?" — the hour, the object, the movement, the room. See
`compliance.md`. This is not only a policy rule; it is the craft rule. "3 a.m.
Third time." outperforms "Trouble sleeping?" because it is specific, and because
the reader does the work of recognising themselves, which is more convincing
than being told.

Every formula in the library is written in a situation-first form. When you
adapt one, keep it there.

## The formula library

Sixty structures across ten families. They are scaffolding for a blank page, not
a menu to pick from. A hook that is recognisably a formula has failed the
recognition test.

### Family 1 — The timestamp

Anchor the ad in a specific moment. The single most reliable opener for a
problem that recurs, and the safest in a restricted category, because a clock is
not a personal attribute.

1. `<Time>. <The recurring event>.`
2. `<Time>. <Event>. Again.`
3. `It is <time> and <the thing that is happening>.`
4. `Every <day of week>, around <time>.`
5. `The <ordinal> time tonight.`
6. `<Season/month>, and <the annual version of the problem>.`

### Family 2 — The object

Let a physical object carry the whole situation. Very strong for a static,
because the image and the line can be the same idea from two directions.

7. `The <object> is on the floor again.`
8. `You already own the <workaround object>. That is the problem.`
9. `Three <objects> and none of them <does the thing>.`
10. `What is under your <place> right now.`
11. `<Object> at <price>. Replaced <N> times this year.`
12. `The <object> is not the problem. <The thing about it> is.`

### Family 3 — The failed workaround

Only for a **workaround-aware** reader. To anyone else this is a second-order
subject and it will not land — see the workaround test in
`intake.md`.

13. `<The workaround> works. For about <duration>.`
14. `You have tried <workaround>. It moves.`
15. `<Workaround> was never designed to <the job you gave it>.`
16. `Nothing wrong with <workaround>. It just does not stay.`
17. `The reason <workaround> stops working by <time>.`
18. `Everyone does <workaround>. Almost nobody does it twice.`

### Family 4 — The reversal

Tell them not to buy, or contradict the category. Buys attention cheaply, so it
is easy to overuse; one per set at most, and it has to actually be true.

19. `Do not buy this <product>.`
20. `Do not buy this if <the genuine disqualifier>.`
21. `<Common advice> is why <the problem persists>.`
22. `We removed <the feature everyone advertises>.`
23. `This is not <the category everyone would file it under>.`
24. `<N> people returned it. Here is what they said.`
25. `It only does one thing.`

### Family 5 — The mechanism

For **solution-aware** readers, who need to know why yours is different, not
that a solution exists.

26. `It stays because of <the specific mechanism>.`
27. `<Mechanism> is the only part that matters.`
28. `The difference is <a physical detail>, and that is the whole difference.`
29. `Why <competing approach> cannot <the outcome>.`
30. `<Material/component>, held by <fastening>. That is it.`
31. `Same <thing>. Different <the one part that changed>.`

### Family 6 — The demonstration

The hook is the picture; the words label it. Strongest format when the product
does something visible.

32. `Watch what happens at <time marker>.`
33. `<Duration> later. Same position.`
34. `Left: <state A>. Right: <state B>.` *(check `compliance.md` before any
    before-and-after involving a body)*
35. `Shake it.`
36. `We left it there for <duration>.`
37. `No edit. One take.`

### Family 7 — The specific number

A precise number reads as measured; a round number reads as marketing. Every
number here needs a source before it ships — see `output-standard.md`.

38. `<Precise N> <units>. That is the entire <thing>.`
39. `<N> of <M> <people> said the same sentence.`
40. `It took <N> versions to get <the detail> right.`
41. `<N> grams. You will not feel it.`
42. `Made <N> at a time. That is the constraint.`

### Family 8 — The address

Speak to a named situation, not a named condition. The line after the colon has
to be a situation, or this family walks straight into the personal-attributes
trap.

43. `If <situation>, this is for you.`
44. `For anyone who <does the specific physical thing>.`
45. `This is for the <role> who <the specific recurring moment>.`
46. `<Occupation/situation>: <the thing only they experience>.`
47. `You will recognise this or you will not.`

### Family 9 — The confession

First-person, small, and true. Carries UGC and founder ads. Dies instantly if
it is invented, because invented confessions all sound the same.

48. `I did not think this would work.`
49. `I bought <N> of these before <the one that worked>.`
50. `I was about to return it.`
51. `Nobody told me <the practical detail>.`
52. `I have been doing this wrong for <duration>.`
53. `My <family member> has stolen mine.`

### Family 10 — The open loop

Withhold one thing, deliberately. Only earns its place if the payoff arrives
inside the ad; an unclosed loop is a click that bounces.

54. `The <object> is not what you think it is.`
55. `There is one thing about this we do not advertise.`
56. `The reason this exists is <a pause> not what you would guess.`
57. `We changed one thing. It was not the <obvious thing>.`
58. `Skip to <time marker> if you already <know the setup>.`
59. `The last <N> seconds is the part that matters.`
60. `Here is the part the photos do not show.`

## Hook research from the market

The library is a fallback. The real source is the market, in this order:

1. **Reviews of the workaround.** One-star and three-star reviews of the thing
   your buyer uses today. The three-star reviews are the richest: they liked it
   and it still failed them, and they explain exactly where. That sentence is
   your hook.
2. **The scrape.** Competitor first lines, verbatim, from `ads.csv`. You are
   looking for the shape that recurs across advertisers, and for the angle
   nobody is running.
3. **Your own support inbox and reviews**, if the product is live. The way a
   customer describes what changed is better copy than anything you will write
   about it.
4. **Forums and community threads** where people describe the problem to each
   other rather than to a brand.

Record the source next to each hook. A hook lifted verbatim from a customer
carries an evidence label; one you invented carries `hypothesis`. They are not
the same asset and should not be ranked in the same column.

---

## Scoring a hook

Score each on five axes, 0 to 2. Total out of 10. The score is a
**judgement**, labelled `hypothesis`, never a prediction.

| Axis | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Specificity** | could open an ad in any category | specific to the category | specific to this situation, in a way only someone who lives it would write |
| **Recognition speed** | needs the second line to make sense | lands by the end of the line | lands on the first three words |
| **Situation, not person** | asserts a personal attribute — **automatic disqualification** | describes a state loosely | pure situation: time, object, movement, room |
| **Channel fit** | works only if you read all of it | works with sound off | visual and text do different jobs and both work alone |
| **Truth** | needs a claim nobody can source | true, sourceable | true, sourced, and specific enough to be checkable |

Any hook scoring 0 on **situation** is cut, not rewritten down. Any hook
scoring 0 on **truth** is cut or sourced. Neither is a ranking question.

Below 6, rewrite. At 6 or 7, it is a variant, not a lead creative. At 8 and
above, it can carry an ad set.

## How many, and how they differ

Produce **`coverage.angles_per_ad_set` distinct angles**, and for each angle
three hooks that differ in **one** thing (`test.one_change_rule`):

- A: the reference.
- B: same angle, different opening move (timestamp instead of object, say).
- C: same angle, same move, different **length** — one short, one that runs
  into a second line.

Three hooks that differ in two things each produce a result you cannot apply to
anything. Three that differ in one produce a finding.

Check angle distinctness with `coverage.angle_distinctness` before counting:
two hooks sharing their main claim **and** their reason are one angle wearing
two hats, and an ad set built from them tests nothing.

## Output

```markdown
## Angle 1 — <the argument, one sentence>
Awareness level: <...>    Moment of recognition: <one sentence>

| Hook | Spoken (video) | On-screen | Opening shot / image | Score | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.A | <≤8 words> | <≤32 chars> | <what it shows, and whether it exists> | 8 | notes |
| 1.B | ... | ... | ... | 7 | hypothesis |
| 1.C | ... | ... | ... | 7 | hypothesis |

What separates B from A: <the single changed element>
What separates C from A: <the single changed element>
```

Mark any opening shot that does not exist yet as `needs production`, and rank
it separately from the hooks that can ship this week. A perfect hook that needs
a shoot is not competing with one that needs an afternoon.
