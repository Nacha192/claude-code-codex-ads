# Copywriting and hooks

## Build the argument before the sentences

State the buyer's present situation, the belief that prevents action, the offer's relevant mechanism, credible proof and the next step. Build a friction inventory from actual reviews, interviews, support, sales calls or public discussion; three well-supported tensions beat fifteen invented ones. Where evidence is thin, mark a creative hypothesis and design a research question.

Distinguish a hook from a benefit label. "Memory foam with a strap" describes construction. A recognizable moment or an honest disqualifier may make the buyer care about that construction. The hook must pay off in the body and destination; curiosity is not permission to mislead.

## Hook families

Choose families from the niche, awareness and proof. Do not force all ten into every campaign.

| Family | Use when | Deliverable constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Recognizable moment | Buyers share a specific situation | Show a concrete scene grounded in research |
| Failed workaround | A common substitute has a demonstrated limitation | Explain the limitation without inventing a universal failure |
| Misdiagnosed cause | Evidence supports a different explanation | Do not assert medical or causal certainty from intuition |
| Demonstration | A visible action or state establishes the benefit | The image must actually show the stated property |
| Honest exclusion | Some buyers are genuinely poor fits | Exclusion must be true and respectful |
| Objection first | A recurring concern blocks action | Answer with proof, not reassurance alone |
| Mechanism reveal | The mechanism is distinctive and comprehensible | Link the mechanism to a supported outcome |
| Cost of friction | Time/money waste is documented | Show assumptions for calculations; no invented savings |
| Offer clarity | Warm audiences understand the category | Exact terms, eligibility, price and next action |
| Story or contrast | A real sequence or fair comparison exists | No fake testimonial, creator identity or staged evidence |

For an initial batch, propose 3 materially different concepts with 2 hooks each unless the user specifies another count. Variants should change the opening situation or argument, not merely swap adjectives. Keep one variable stable for an intentional A/B comparison.

## Draft and edit

Compose directly in the target language using source vocabulary. Confirm accent/register when it matters; preserve currencies, units, idioms and product names. Read copy aloud. Back-translation can check meaning but cannot certify naturalness. Ask a fluent reviewer.

Use PAS, AIDA or a demonstration sequence as optional scaffolding; remove visible formula language. Prefer verbs, objects and situations over vague superlatives. Remove filler, arbitrary urgency, unsupported certainty and AI-flavored transitions. Do not turn every headline into a product specification to make it "safe".

Review separately: strategic fit; evidence; clarity; spoken rhythm; offer/CTA continuity; language; platform context. For each consequential edit identify the original issue and an actual replacement. Do not manufacture a numeric conversion prediction or impersonate famous copywriters as reviewers.

## Creative judgment

Apply a competitor-substitution test: if any brand can say the same thing, look for a more relevant proof, situation or voice. It is a prompt for improvement, not proof that common category benefits are forbidden. Apply a visual-complement test: the image should reveal, demonstrate or tension the words rather than duplicate them. Apply a payoff test: the first screen and destination must resolve the hook honestly.

Record qualitative readiness in five dimensions (buyer recognition, distinctive premise, proof, readability, action continuity) with specific evidence and repairs. Unsubstantiated claims fail regardless of a high average score. Stop polishing when the concept meets the brief and remaining uncertainty can only be resolved by real testing.

Output hook, primary text, headline, optional description, CTA, proof references, awareness stage, language, visual direction and test hypothesis. Treat recommended mobile preview lengths separately from actual API/placement limits; verify limits before upload.
