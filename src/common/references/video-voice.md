# Video, motion, UGC and voice direction

## Choose the production route

Use authentic footage for real people, demonstrations and evidence; deterministic motion for diagrams, offers, interface walkthroughs and precise typography; generated clips for approved illustrative scenes. Combine routes when useful. Blender is appropriate for justified 3D shots when available; a model's Blender skill does not establish advertising effectiveness. Do not add particles or transitions with no narrative job.

Analyze a reference at timecodes: first frame, hook, cuts, shot scales, text hierarchy, narration, music, effects, proof and CTA. Transfer timing and storytelling principles, not a competitor's identity, voice, copyrighted footage or distinctive copy. Label unavailable audio/video inspection honestly.

## Script to storyboard

Make a timed table: scene ID, start/end seconds, visual action, camera/composition, on-screen words, voice line, sound cue, proof reference, asset source and intended viewer inference. Plan one persuasive arc that fits the requested duration. As a starting sketch, a 30-second service ad might use 0–3 seconds for a recognizable friction, 3–10 for the stakes, 10–20 for mechanism/proof, 20–26 for qualification and 26–30 for the next step. Change this when the idea needs it.

Create a simple animatic first within the approved production scope. Lock narration timing before expensive final motion; use real generated audio duration rather than word-count promises. Reflow after voice changes. Keep separate audio stems for voice, music and effects.

## Voice card

Record target language, regional accent, perceived age/timbre without impersonation, conversational energy, warmth, authority, pace, pauses, emphasis, pronunciation lexicon and unwanted mannerisms. Tie direction to the buyer and offer: a confident trade-service explanation needs different delivery from intimate product storytelling. "More human" means appropriate phrasing and performance, not random breaths or an imitation of a real person.

Preview a few existing voices without generating when possible. After approval, test 2–3 short readings of the same difficult passage within the batch ceiling; compare intelligibility, accent, pronunciation, intent and fatigue. Select by listening, not model prestige or price. Use consented/licensed voices; never fabricate creator experience or clone a person without permission. Do not treat a synthetic UGC performer as a real customer testimonial. Verify applicable disclosure and commercial-use terms.

Use only controls supported by the chosen voice model. Emotion tags, stability, similarity and speed differ across providers; unsupported instructions may be spoken literally. Record the actual voice/model/settings used. A provider's voice-agent management tool is not automatically a text-to-speech tool.

Superwhisper is useful for dictating briefs or transcribing source speech; do not present dictation as voice generation. Route generated narration to a verified TTS tool such as ElevenLabs or an available OpenAI speech tool.

## Timing and audio QA

Estimate speech fit before generation, then replace estimates with measured durations. Treat syllable/word-rate heuristics as language-specific warnings. If a line is rushed, shorten it or lengthen the scene rather than automatically accelerating it. Leave comprehension time after important text; a rough 0.3–0.4 seconds per word is a planning hint, not an accessibility guarantee.

Listen end to end. Check mispronunciations, cut syllables, glitches, repeated words, unnatural stresses and excessive pauses. Keep voice intelligible over music; inspect clipping and loudness using available audio tools. No universal LUFS target is implied: choose delivery requirements and document the mix. Review sound-off with captions, sound-on on speakers/headphones, and at small-screen size. Captions must match the final take, not an obsolete script.

Bind visual evidence to claims: if narration promises a feature, show it or a clearly labeled illustration; do not use decorative stock as proof. Referential continuity is a review question, not a rule that every spoken noun must be literal footage. Capture beginning, transitions, peak overlays and ending, then watch the full render for temporal defects.

## Deliverables

Provide final video(s), cover image where useful, captions/transcript, final script, source project, voice/settings manifest, asset rights notes and concise QA evidence. MP4/H.264/AAC is a common compatibility route; inspect actual placement and client requirements. Probe duration, dimensions, frame rate and audio streams. Open the exported file. "Script ready" and "video rendered without voice" are incomplete statuses when the user requested a finished voiced ad.
